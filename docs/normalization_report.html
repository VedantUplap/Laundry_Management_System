Database Normalization Report (3NF)

Project: Laundry Service Management System (LSMS) Target: Third Normal Form (3NF) Database: MySQL 8.0+ Schema Tables: 12 Relational Entities

1. Introduction

This report documents the rigorous mathematical database normalization process applied to the Laundry Service Management System (LSMS). The design progresses from an Unnormalized Form (UNF) through First Normal Form (1NF), Second Normal Form (2NF), and reaches a fully optimized Third Normal Form (3NF).

Primary Objectives:

  • Eliminate data redundancy and duplicate storage.
  • Prevent Insertion, Update, and Deletion Anomalies.
  • Enforce referential integrity and strict multi-table relationships.
  • Provide an immutable audit trail for historical laundry orders, services, and financial payments.
UNF (Flat Table)   ──►   1NF (Atomic Values)   ──►   2NF (No Partial FDs)   ──►   3NF (No Transitive FDs)

2. Unnormalized Form (UNF)

In the initial conceptual model, all business data is captured in a single flat record with repeating groups and multi-valued attributes:

LAUNDRY_ORDER (
  OrderID, OrderDate, PickupDate, DeliveryDate, OrderStatus,
  CustomerID, CustomerFirstName, CustomerLastName, CustomerPhone, CustomerEmail, CustomerAddress,
  [ServiceID, ServiceName, ServiceDescription, PricePerKg, GarmentType, Quantity, WeightKg, ServiceCost],  ← Repeating Group
  TotalWeight, TotalCost,
  BillID, BillDate, DueDate, BillAmount, BillStatus,
  [PaymentID, PaymentDate, AmountPaid, PaymentMethod, TransactionID],  ← Repeating Group
  AgentID, AgentName, AgentPhone, VehicleNumber,
  DeliveryID, PickupTime, DeliveryTime, DeliveryStatus,
  FeedbackID, Rating, Comments, FeedbackDate,
  [ItemID, ItemName, Unit, QuantityUsed, QuantityAvailable, ReorderLevel]  ← Repeating Group
)

Severe Anomalies Identified:

  • Repeating Groups: An order can have multiple garments/services, multiple partial payments, and multiple inventory items consumed.
  • Update Anomaly: Changing a customer's address or phone requires modifying every historical order record.
  • Insertion Anomaly: A new laundry service or delivery agent cannot be added without creating a dummy order.
  • Deletion Anomaly: Deleting an order accidentally erases the customer profile or inventory records.

3. First Normal Form (1NF)

Rules Applied:

  • Eliminate all repeating groups and nested tables.
  • Ensure all column attributes contain atomic (single-valued) items.
  • Define primary keys for unique record identification.
LAUNDRY_ORDER (OrderID PK, OrderDate, PickupDate, DeliveryDate, Status,
               CustomerID, CustomerFirstName, CustomerLastName, CustomerPhone,
               CustomerEmail, CustomerAddress, TotalWeight, TotalCost,
               BillID, BillDate, DueDate, BillAmount, BillStatus,
               AgentID, AgentName, AgentPhone, VehicleNumber,
               DeliveryStatus, PickupTime, DeliveryTime,
               FeedbackRating, FeedbackComments, FeedbackDate)

ORDER_DETAIL (DetailID PK, OrderID, ServiceID, ServiceName, ServiceDescription,
              PricePerKg, GarmentType, Quantity, WeightKg, ServiceCost)

PAYMENT (PaymentID PK, OrderID, BillID, PaymentDate, AmountPaid,
         PaymentMethod, TransactionID)

USAGE_LOG (UsageID PK, OrderID, ItemID, ItemName, Unit,
           QuantityUsed, QuantityAvailable, ReorderLevel, UsageDate)

1NF Status: SATISFIED All multi-valued fields removed; all column values are atomic.

4. Second Normal Form (2NF)

Rule Applied: Remove all Partial Functional Dependencies — every non-key attribute must be fully functionally dependent on the primary key.

Partial Dependencies Removed:

  • In ORDER_DETAIL: {ServiceName, Description, PricePerKg} depend only on ServiceID, not DetailID. Extracted to SERVICE_TYPE.
  • In USAGE_LOG: {ItemName, Unit, QuantityAvailable, ReorderLevel} depend only on ItemID. Extracted to INVENTORY.
  • In LAUNDRY_ORDER: Customer details depend solely on CustomerID; agent details depend on AgentID; bills depend on BillID. Extracted into dedicated entity tables.
CUSTOMER (CustomerID PK, FirstName, LastName, Phone, Email, Address)
SERVICE_TYPE (ServiceID PK, ServiceName, Description, PricePerKg)
INVENTORY (ItemID PK, ItemName, Unit, QuantityAvailable, ReorderLevel, CostPerUnit)
DELIVERY_AGENT (AgentID PK, AgentName, Phone, VehicleNumber)
BILLING (BillID PK, OrderID FK, BillDate, DueDate, TotalAmount, BillStatus)
ORDERS (OrderID PK, CustomerID FK, OrderDate, PickupDate, DeliveryDate, Status, TotalWeight, TotalCost)
ORDER_DETAILS (DetailID PK, OrderID FK, ServiceID FK, GarmentType, Quantity, WeightKg, ServiceCost)
PAYMENT (PaymentID PK, BillID FK, PaymentDate, AmountPaid, PaymentMethod, TransactionID)
USAGE_LOG (UsageID PK, OrderID FK, ItemID FK, QuantityUsed, UsageDate)
DELIVERY (DeliveryID PK, OrderID FK, AgentID FK, PickupTime, DeliveryTime, DeliveryStatus)
FEEDBACK (FeedbackID PK, CustomerID FK, OrderID FK, Rating, Comments, FeedbackDate)

2NF Status: SATISFIED No partial dependencies exist.

5. Third Normal Form (3NF)

Rule Applied: Remove all Transitive Functional Dependencies — non-key attributes must not depend on other non-key attributes ($X \rightarrow Y$ where neither is a key).

Transitive Dependencies Resolved:

  • Authentication vs. Domain Separation: In the CUSTOMER and DELIVERY_AGENT tables, storing Password and Role creates a transitive dependency on Email.
    Resolution: Created the independent USERS(UserID PK, Username, Email, Password, Role) entity. CUSTOMER and DELIVERY_AGENT simply reference UserID FK.
  • Historical Cost Immutability: In ORDER_DETAILS, ServiceCost is calculated as $\text{WeightKg} \times \text{PricePerKg}$ at order placement time and stored to protect against future price modifications in SERVICE_TYPE. 
-- Final 3NF Normalized Entities:
USERS (UserID PK, Username UNIQUE, Email UNIQUE, Password, Role, IsActive)
CUSTOMER (CustomerID PK, UserID FK→USERS, FirstName, LastName, Phone, Address)
SERVICE_TYPE (ServiceID PK, ServiceName UNIQUE, Description, PricePerKg, IsActive)
ORDERS (OrderID PK, CustomerID FK→CUSTOMER, OrderDate, PickupDate, DeliveryDate, Status, TotalWeight, TotalCost, Notes)
ORDER_DETAILS (DetailID PK, OrderID FK→ORDERS, ServiceID FK→SERVICE_TYPE, GarmentType, Quantity, WeightKg, ServiceCost)
BILLING (BillID PK, OrderID FK→ORDERS UNIQUE, BillDate, DueDate, TotalAmount, BillStatus)
PAYMENT (PaymentID PK, BillID FK→BILLING, PaymentDate, AmountPaid, PaymentMethod, TransactionID)
INVENTORY (ItemID PK, ItemName UNIQUE, Unit, QuantityAvailable, ReorderLevel, CostPerUnit)
USAGE_LOG (UsageID PK, OrderID FK→ORDERS, ItemID FK→INVENTORY, QuantityUsed, UsageDate)
DELIVERY_AGENT (AgentID PK, UserID FK→USERS, AgentName, Phone, VehicleNumber, IsActive)
DELIVERY (DeliveryID PK, OrderID FK→ORDERS UNIQUE, AgentID FK→DELIVERY_AGENT, PickupTime, DeliveryTime, DeliveryStatus)
FEEDBACK (FeedbackID PK, CustomerID FK→CUSTOMER, OrderID FK→ORDERS UNIQUE, Rating CHECK(1-5), Comments, FeedbackDate)

3NF Status: SATISFIED All non-key attributes depend solely and directly on the primary key.

7. Functional Dependencies (FD Set)

  • USERS: UserID → {Username, Email, Password, Role, IsActive}
  • CUSTOMER: CustomerID → {UserID, FirstName, LastName, Phone, Address}
  • SERVICE_TYPE: ServiceID → {ServiceName, Description, PricePerKg, IsActive}
  • ORDERS: OrderID → {CustomerID, OrderDate, PickupDate, DeliveryDate, Status, TotalWeight, TotalCost, Notes}
  • ORDER_DETAILS: DetailID → {OrderID, ServiceID, GarmentType, Quantity, WeightKg, ServiceCost}
  • BILLING: BillID → {OrderID, BillDate, DueDate, TotalAmount, BillStatus} and OrderID → BillID
  • PAYMENT: PaymentID → {BillID, PaymentDate, AmountPaid, PaymentMethod, TransactionID}
  • INVENTORY: ItemID → {ItemName, Unit, QuantityAvailable, ReorderLevel, CostPerUnit}
  • USAGE_LOG: UsageID → {OrderID, ItemID, QuantityUsed, UsageDate}
  • DELIVERY_AGENT: AgentID → {UserID, AgentName, Phone, VehicleNumber, IsActive}
  • DELIVERY: DeliveryID → {OrderID, AgentID, PickupTime, DeliveryTime, DeliveryStatus} and OrderID → DeliveryID
  • FEEDBACK: FeedbackID → {CustomerID, OrderID, Rating, Comments, FeedbackDate} and OrderID → FeedbackID

8. Database Triggers & Views (Derived Redundancy Elimination)

To maintain 3NF while providing instantaneous reporting performance:

  • Automatic Order Recalculation Triggers: trg_recalculate_order_after_insert/update/delete automatically updates ORDERS.TotalWeight and ORDERS.TotalCost whenever order line items change.
  • Inventory Consumption Trigger: trg_reduce_inventory_after_usage automatically subtracts stock from INVENTORY when a usage record is logged.
  • 6 Analytical Views: customer_order_history, pending_deliveries, outstanding_payments, inventory_status, service_popularity, and daily_revenue execute clean multi-table JOINs without denormalizing base tables.

9. Conclusion

The database schema implemented in database/schema.sql and utilized throughout the Laundry Service Management System strictly satisfies all requirements of Third Normal Form (3NF). It eliminates data anomalies, prevents data duplication, and guarantees complete referential and transactional integrity.
